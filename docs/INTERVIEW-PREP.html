00:45 — 01:15 · Position + chart

Show: position card showing 17 YES · spot chart drawing from Pyth Hermes polls
The chart you see is the underlying — live Pyth Hermes feed, polling every 5 seconds. Same Pyth feed that settles the market on-chain at 4 PM. One oracle for live and settle eliminates the read/settle price drift problem.

01:15 — 02:00 · Flip to desktop

Show: desktop matrix → trade page with full order book → admin page
Desktop is the same Next.js app, same wallet adapter, same on-chain reads. Order book is rendered directly from the on-chain account — not a backend cache. Admin page is for diagnostics and settlement nudging; it doesn't have any privileged write paths the program enforces.

02:00 — 02:30 · Architecture beat

Three sentences, one each:
Solana for cost and the mobile wallet ecosystem. Custom in-program CLOB because Phoenix's devnet deploy was permissioned and we pivoted to control our own upgrade path on day four. Pyth for both live and settle — non-custodial vault with no admin withdraw instruction means the only way money leaves is through a winning redeem or a force-redeem after the grace window.

2. Defense Q&A2.5 min

Most likely questions, in rough order of probability. Pick the strongest one-liner and stop talking — they will reroute if they want more.
Why Solana, not an L2? expected
Three reasons in order: cost — $0.0001 per state-changing tx makes 0DTE economically possible at retail size; Pyth — Pyth was built for Solana and Pythnet, it's first-class here, not a bridged afterthought; mobile wallets — Phantom and Solflare own the mobile UX, and a same-day options product lives or dies on phone trading. An L2 would force me to bridge USDC and use a worse mobile wallet story for a feature I don't need: programmable settlement.
Why a custom CLOB instead of Phoenix or Drift? expected
Original plan was Phoenix. Day four discovered Phoenix's devnet deploy is permissioned — couldn't reproduce the market-creation path without their team. Pivot was documented as DR-020. Binary outcome books are thin by nature, so an in-program CLOB matches the actual liquidity shape and gives me full control of the upgrade path. Phoenix is documented as a v1.1 swap — the value isn't auto-liquidity, it's distribution and discovery: MMs already wired into Phoenix can opt-in to our books without building a custom integration, and our markets surface to the existing Phoenix user base. And a Phoenix listing makes us aggregator-eligible — Jupiter and similar see depth they can route to, which is the actual liquidity-bootstrap mechanism on Solana.
What happens if Pyth lies or stalls? expected
Two defenses in the program. Staleness: settle reverts if the Pyth publish_time is older than the configured staleness window. Confidence: settle reverts if Pyth's confidence band is wider than 50bps of price. If both gates fail, the admin override path opens with a 24-hour delay and an on-chain reason field — the user has time to redeem-pair out before any disputed settle is applied.
What's your trust model? Who can move user funds? expected
No one. There is no withdraw_to_admin instruction in the program — that's the mechanical, not policy, guarantee. The vault PDA only releases USDC through redeem (winning token burn) or redeem_pair (both sides burn together pre-settle). After the grace window, anyone can call force_redeem for any holder, recovering vault rent.
What if your cron dies at 4 PM? very likely
Settle is permissionless. The cron on Trigger.dev is a convenience nudger, not the authority — anyone can crank settle_market after expiry. The program checks Pyth and writes the outcome idempotently, so races between callers cost the loser a transaction fee but produce the same outcome. DR-002 codifies this — cron failure is not market failure.
How does this scale? Rent on Solana adds up. expected
Per market: 0.128 SOL verified on-chain — the OrderBook account alone is 16,448 bytes / 0.115 SOL (90.4% of the total). At 49 markets/day that's ~6.3 SOL/day at full grid. Rent recovery is a documented pre-mainnet precondition in specs/deferred.md — v1.1 adds force_cancel_order + close_order_book instructions that recover 95.2% of per-market rent, dropping net stranded to ~$1/market.
How much did your CLOB cost vs Phoenix? expected
Ours is cheaper per market, not more expensive — that's actually a defense of the DR-020 pivot. Ours: 16,448 bytes → 0.115 SOL ≈ $18 per market, verified on-chain. Phoenix at the standard 500/500/100 sizing (bids/asks/seats) is ~80KB → ~0.5–0.6 SOL per market — 4–5× our cost. And no pre-existing Phoenix market exists for our YES/USDC or NO/USDC mints, so we'd have been the ones initializing one per strike per day — paying the full ~0.5 SOL each, not amortizing across other integrators. Source for the 500/500/100 number: specs/clob-strategy.md §"InitializeMarket CPI". The pivot cost us audit-pedigree on the matching engine; it bought us better unit economics and full control of the upgrade path.
MEV and front-running? if quant lens
No AMM curve to sandwich. Orders rest at fixed prices on a CLOB, takers consume them at the resting price, and the buy paths are IOC — leftover quantity rejects instead of resting against you. The close-of-day Pyth read is permissionless and idempotent; racing for it doesn't change the price.
How do you bootstrap liquidity? likely
Demo uses an automation seeder posting both sides at fair-coin-flip prices. Production has two levers: creator rebates in DR-008 — the wallet that created the market gets a slice of mint fees from every trade against it — and a per-ticker market-maker integration as a v1.1 partnership. Binary outcome books have an obvious anchor (50¢/50¢) which makes algorithmic MM straightforward.
Why bUSDC, not USDC? technical
Devnet only. Real USDC mint doesn't exist on devnet, so we ship a self-controlled mint with the same 6-decimal layout and call it bUSDC. MarketConfig.usdc_mint is parameterized — flipping to mainnet USDC is a one-instruction config update; no program redeploy.
Why daily expiry (0DTE) instead of weekly? product
Three reasons. Oracle simplicity: one Pyth read per cycle, no overnight risk window to model. Retail mental model: "will it close above this today" is a question anyone watching the market already asks. Capital velocity: 0DTE flow in TradFi is the fastest-growing options segment — daily turn is the product-market fit, not weekly tail risk.
What did you defer and why? very likely
Four big ones, all in specs/deferred.md: rent-recovery instructions (pre-mainnet hard gate), Phoenix swap (v1.1 if MM depth becomes the bottleneck), WebSocket subscription path (Vercel serverless can't proxy WS — needs a Cloudflare Worker proxy in production; demo polls at 5s), and Neon-backed market registry (replaces the hardcoded demo registry; currently I list known PDAs to dodge a Helius getProgramAccounts restriction).
What would you do differently with another week? soft
Three things, in priority. One: ship the rent-recovery instructions — the only thing actually blocking mainnet. Two: replace the demo registry with the Neon-backed pattern + a backend GPA sync job. Three: Phoenix evaluation — if we can get a non-permissioned deploy path, Phoenix buys us distribution and discovery: existing MMs that already speak Phoenix's protocol can opt-in to our books, and our markets show up in the Phoenix-integrated frontends and aggregators that users already use. A Phoenix listing also makes us aggregator-eligible — Jupiter and similar see depth they can route to, which is the actual liquidity-bootstrap mechanism on Solana.
